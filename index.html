    TLB / Guide

    Study Guide

    How to use this hub — study workflow and evidence-based learning methods.

    Recommended study workflow

    1. Read the notes for a topic, then work through the matching tutorial.
    2. Attempt the mock quiz without notes and use the instant feedback to find weak spots.
    3. Re-read the notes for questions you missed, then drill them with “Retry wrong only”.
    4. Compare your scores with past grade distributions to calibrate your target.

    Evidence-based learning methods

    • Active recall — test yourself before you feel ready.
    • Spaced repetition — review at expanding intervals (1, 3, 7 days).
    • Interleaving — mix topics instead of practising one at a time.
    • Self-explanation — put each concept into your own words.

    Mock quiz features

    • Instant correct/wrong feedback with explanations on every question.
    • Keyboard shortcuts: press 1–4 to answer, Enter to continue.
    • Timer, shuffled options and attempt history (best score kept).
    • “Retry wrong only” focuses practice on your weakest questions.
    • Filter the bank by topic to drill one subject at a time.

    Reading the grade distribution

    • Each bar shows the share of students in a grade band.
    • D and above counts as a pass; bands sort from A+ down to F.
    • Use the cohort median as your benchmark, not the maximum.

    Exam strategy

    • Scan the whole paper for the first minute before answering anything.
    • Answer easy questions first; flag the hard ones and return later.
    • In MCQs, eliminate two options before choosing — turn guessing into deciding.
    • Keep a running clock: about one mark per minute is a sound default.

    Wellbeing during exams

    • Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep in the exam week — memory consolidates during sleep.
    • Take short movement breaks every 45–60 minutes of study.
    • Stay hydrated and eat regular meals; caffeine after noon can cost you sleep.
    Tip: ten minutes of retrieval practice beats an hour of re-reading. Study in short, focused sessions.
